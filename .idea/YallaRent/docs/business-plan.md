# Business Plan - YallaRent

## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1 Description

YallaRent is a student equipment rental and sharing platform for Algerian campuses. It combines:
- Company-owned inventory rental,
- Peer-to-peer student listings,
- Campus-adjacent pickup/return hubs,
- Optional delivery, protection, and maintenance services.

The model addresses temporary-use demand in a large university population (1,530,230 enrolled students in 2024/2025) [S09](./sources.md#s09).

### 1.2 Business Overview and Objectives

**Business objective (5-year):** build a pilot-proven, campus-scalable rental infrastructure with no commission on P2P transactions [A28](./assumptions.md#a28).

**Primary objectives:**
1. Pilot and validate unit economics in AY 2026/2027.
2. Reach operational break-even from Year 2 (base case) [A27](./assumptions.md#a27).
3. Expand from 1 campus to 18 campuses by Year 5 [A06](./assumptions.md#a06).
4. Build mixed revenue resilience across 7 streams [A31](./assumptions.md#a31) to [A37](./assumptions.md#a37).

### 1.3 Product and Value Proposition

**For students:** temporary access to essential equipment without heavy upfront purchase.

**For student providers:** monetization of idle assets with verification and trust tooling.

**For universities:** partner-ready service layer that improves student living support.

Digital channel feasibility is strong with 36.2 million internet users (76.9%) and 54.8 million mobile connections (116%) in early 2025 [S16](./sources.md#s16), [S15](./sources.md#s15).

### 1.4 Market and Customers

- TAM baseline: 1,530,230 enrolled students [S09](./sources.md#s09).
- Targeted initial SOM (active renters): 1,200 in AY 2026/2027 [A07](./assumptions.md#a07).
- Year-5 active renters target: 47,500 [A07](./assumptions.md#a07).

### 1.5 Strategy and Development

- **Phase 1 (AY26/27):** 1-campus pilot with controlled SKU scope.
- **Phase 2 (AY27/28 to AY28/29):** 3 to 7 campuses, stronger partner contracts.
- **Phase 3 (AY29/30 to AY30/31):** 12 to 18 campuses, full multi-stream model.

## 3. Products / Services

### 3.1 Product Overview

1. Company-owned item rentals.
2. P2P listings (free up to 3 items, paid plans above threshold) [A14](./assumptions.md#a14), [A28](./assumptions.md#a28).
3. Delivery and pickup options [A10](./assumptions.md#a10), [A11](./assumptions.md#a11).
4. Damage protection add-on [A12](./assumptions.md#a12), [A13](./assumptions.md#a13).
5. Maintenance and end-cycle resale [A18](./assumptions.md#a18) to [A21](./assumptions.md#a21).

### 3.2 Technical Characteristics and Quality

- Mobile-first UX required by local connectivity context [S15](./sources.md#s15), [S16](./sources.md#s16).
- Performance target informed by local median speeds (23.42 Mbps mobile; 15.05 Mbps fixed) [S18](./sources.md#s18), [S19](./sources.md#s19).
- Role-based admin controls and auditable event logs for trust and compliance.

### 3.3 Product Strengths and Competitive Advantages

- Campus-specific logistics workflow versus generic classified platforms [S26](./sources.md#s26), [S27](./sources.md#s27).
- Mixed revenue engine without P2P commission dependency [A28](./assumptions.md#a28).
- Built-in refurbishment/resale loop for inventory lifecycle control [A20](./assumptions.md#a20), [A21](./assumptions.md#a21).

### 3.4 Client Purchase Factors

- Total cost transparency.
- Trust in item condition and identity checks.
- Speed and convenience of handover.
- Availability at semester-critical dates.

### 3.5 Product Portfolio and Revenue Contribution (Base Case)

| Stream | AY26/27 Share | AY30/31 Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Company-owned rentals | 60.0% | 51.0% |
| Delivery fees | 3.5% | 4.1% |
| Damage protection | 3.6% | 4.5% |
| Subscription plans | 8.3% | 13.9% |
| University partnerships | 13.9% | 17.3% |
| Maintenance | 2.5% | 2.5% |
| Resale | 8.1% | 6.7% |

### 3.6 Product Development Roadmap

- Year 1: booking core, verification, hub workflow, basic provider dashboard.
- Year 2-3: automation for disputes, SLA dashboarding, improved provider controls.
- Year 4-5: cross-campus inventory optimization and advanced risk scoring.

### 3.7 Market Life Cycle and Seasonality

- Demand peaks around semester starts and exam windows.
- End-of-semester return cycles feed maintenance and resale channels.

### 3.8 Sales Projection Drivers

- Active renters growth [A07](./assumptions.md#a07).
- Orders and average fee growth [A08](./assumptions.md#a08), [A09](./assumptions.md#a09).
- Attach-rate progression [A10](./assumptions.md#a10), [A12](./assumptions.md#a12).

### 3.9 After-Sales Service and Support

- Return inspections and damage workflows.
- Maintenance interventions and replacement path.
- Escalation path for disputes.

## 5. Competition

### 5.1 Local Alternatives

- Ouedkniss: broad classified market [S26](./sources.md#s26).
- OpenSooq: furniture listing channel [S27](./sources.md#s27).
- Dirleco: local rental behavior proof in non-student equipment categories [S28](./sources.md#s28).

### 5.2 International Benchmarks

- CORT student package benchmark starts at $139 and references 12-month lease framing [S29](./sources.md#s29), [S30](./sources.md#s30).

### 5.3 Positioning

YallaRent differentiates on trust + campus logistics + mixed physical/digital service layer, not listing volume alone.

### 5.4 Competitive Response Plan

- Maintain no-commission P2P positioning.
- Expand defensibility through operational SLAs and partner density.
- Use bundle economics and lifecycle controls to protect margins.

## 6. Marketing Plan

### 6.1 Product Strategy

- Focus categories: study productivity and dorm comfort essentials.
- Build repeat behavior through bundles and semester-aligned offers.

### 6.2 Pricing Strategy

- Budget-sensitive architecture due unemployment/inflation environment [S06](./sources.md#s06), [S07](./sources.md#s07).
- Planned fee anchors:
  - Company-owned average order: 1,800 DZD (Y1), rising to 2,200 DZD (Y5) [A09](./assumptions.md#a09).
  - Delivery: 300 to 380 DZD [A11](./assumptions.md#a11).
  - Protection: 180 to 260 DZD [A13](./assumptions.md#a13).
  - Provider plan: 12,000 DZD annual [A15](./assumptions.md#a15).

### 6.3 Communication and Promotion Strategy

- Campus ambassadors and referral loops.
- Mobile-first growth channels aligned with 25.6M social identities [S17](./sources.md#s17).
- Trust-led messaging: verification, condition evidence, and safe returns.

### 6.4 Distribution Strategy

- Digital booking + physical hub fulfillment.
- Optional last-mile delivery for higher-convenience segments.

## 10. Risk Assessment

### 10.1 Internal Risks (Organization, Management, Operations)

- Early-stage execution overload across logistics and support.
- Mitigation: phased campus expansion and SLA-led operating cadence.

### 10.2 Commercial Risks (Market, Product, Pricing)

- Strong substitute pressure from informal/classified channels [S26](./sources.md#s26), [S27](./sources.md#s27).
- Mitigation: trust differentiation and bundle economics.

### 10.3 Financial Risks

- Inflation and cost drift [S07](./sources.md#s07).
- Mitigation: quarterly repricing controls and scenario re-forecasting.

### 10.4 Exogenous Risks (Legal, Political)

- Data-governance failures can create legal exposure under Law 18-07 [S22](./sources.md#s22), [S23](./sources.md#s23).
- Mitigation: consent and retention governance by design.

### 10.5 Technical and Operational Risks

- Device/network heterogeneity despite improving speed trends [S18](./sources.md#s18), [S20](./sources.md#s20).
- Mitigation: mobile performance budget and fallback flows.

### 10.6 Environmental and Ecological Risks

- High damage rates weaken resale sustainability loop.
- Mitigation: inspection protocol + maintenance routing + quality scorecards.

### 10.7 Risk Probability-Impact Matrix (Summary)

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low adoption vs. target | Medium | High | High |
| High damage/loss rate | Medium | High | High |
| Compliance incident | Low-Medium | High | High |
| Supply stockout peak | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| Logistics SLA misses | Medium | Medium | Medium |

### 10.8 Risk Management Philosophy

Operate with conservative pilot controls, data-backed scaling gates, and quarterly scenario recalibration.
